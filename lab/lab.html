Fixed points of a learned short-time operator

A neural operator is fitted to short-step data from a nonlinear system, and the system's steady states are then computed from the operator alone — the equation-free move that motivates the layer, shown end to end.

Abstract

We fit a random-feature operator to one-step pairs of the scalar system dudt=λ−u2 with Δt=0.05, then solve ψ(u)=S(u)−u=0 by Newton's method using only the learned map. Over 91 values of λ the recovered fixed points track the analytic branch u∗=λ with a maximum relative error of 0.0002% (median 0.0000%), and the spectral radius of the learned map stays below one everywhere, so every recovered steady state is attracting. The demonstration is the consumer of framework/surrogate: it shows that system-level quantities — existence, location, stability — can be read from a fitted operator without integrating the governing equation.

Method

  1. Data. 1,200 one-step pairs (ut, ut+Δt) generated by RK4 at Δt=0.05, sampled around the branch.
  2. Operator. Random features φ(u) = tanh(Wu + c) with a ridge fit on the increment, so the regression is conditioned on the short-time dynamics; 256 features, seed 0.
  3. Fixed point. Newton on ψ(u)=S(u)−u=0 with a finite-difference Jacobian and backtracking: uk+1=uk−J−1ψ(uk).
  4. Stability. Eigenvalues of ∂S/∂u at the solution; the state is attracting when ρ(∂S∂u)<1.
  5. Check. The same procedure on the analytic map would return the closed form exactly; the gap between the curves is the operator's error, ε=|ulearned−λ|/λ.

What this does and does not show

Does. A short-time operator fitted on data supports system-level computation: fixed points, their stability, and their parameter dependence — the quantities a scientist wants, without writing the governing equation down.

Does not. It is not a PDE solver, and no claim is made outside the training interval. The operator is a random-feature regressor, not a DeepONet; the state here is one-dimensional and smooth. Applying the machinery to discrete objects such as token sequences would be a category error: they are not C¹ function spaces, and the correct analogue there is a transfer operator.
